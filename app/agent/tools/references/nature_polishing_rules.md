# Nature-Style Academic Polishing Rules

Extracted from nature-polishing v5.0.2 by Yuan1z0825.

## Default Stance

- Language serves argument. Do not polish sentences while leaving reasoning broken.
- Do not invent data, references, mechanisms, or novelty claims.
- If the draft is Chinese or structurally rough, reconstruct logic first, prose second.

## Paper Type Identification

Before editing, determine the paper type:
- `Research paper`: why the phenomenon matters, what was done, what was found, what it means.
- `Methods paper`: whether the method works, is reproducible, and is better under fair comparison.
- `Review paper (Literature review)`: thematic synthesis, gap identification, future directions.

Do not use one narrative logic for all paper types.

## Reader Priority Order

1. Is this relevant to me?
2. What is new here?
3. Do I trust it?
4. Can I reuse it?
5. What does it mean, and where are the boundaries?

## Hourglass Structure

- Introduction: open broadly, narrow to specific gap/question/hypothesis.
- Discussion/Conclusion: widen again, connecting findings back to literature.

## Failure Mode Diagnosis (priority order)

`paper type -> section job -> paragraph logic -> claim/evidence/boundary -> sentence polish`

## Section Responsibilities

### Introduction
- Tell reader why the work matters, what gap it fills, what is known, what remains unresolved.
- State what question the paper asks and how the study addresses it.
- Do NOT summarize Results or Conclusion here.

### Results
- Mainly past tense. Report what was observed, under what conditions, with what quantitative support.
- Answer "what happened", not "what it ultimately means".

### Discussion
- How the work fits within the broader field. What has been added to understanding.
- Whether findings support, complicate, or revise earlier results.
- How findings are interpreted and when interpretation may fail.
- Short rule: Results = what we observed. Discussion = how we understand it, and when it may fail.

### Conclusion
Three-part close: 1. restate central contribution; 2. summarize key evidence; 3. state implication with boundary.
No new data. Always run overclaim check.

### Abstract (mini-paper)
`context/problem -> gap/objective -> approach -> key results -> implication`

## Sentence Rules

- Aim for 10-30 word range. Keep every sentence <= 30 words.
- If >20 words, check if it contains more than one main proposition.
- Split overloaded sentences rather than polishing cosmetically.
- Prefer one core subject-verb proposition per sentence.
- The last sentence of a paragraph is often the weakest — check it explicitly.

## Paragraph Rules

- One controlling idea per paragraph, followed by support.
- Support: data, comparison, explanation, consequence, literature, or limitation.
- New idea = new paragraph.
- Use thematic linking, not repetitive openings.

## Results vs Discussion Vocabulary

Results: was detected, increased, showed, enabled, achieved.
Discussion: may reflect, suggests that, could indicate, is likely due to, may facilitate.

## AI Traffic-Light Boundary

- Green (acceptable with verification): improve grammar/clarity, generate outline options, produce alternative phrasings, summarize literature for categorization.
- Yellow (strong human control): explain methods/results wording, draft reviewer-response frameworks, code/statistics explanations.
- Red (inappropriate): draft core argument from scratch, insert AI-generated references without checking, upload unpublished manuscripts to public models.

## Output Format

1. Polished text as plain prose.
2. Revision notes: 3-5 short bullets on major structural and stylistic changes.
3. If section logic changed, say so explicitly.
