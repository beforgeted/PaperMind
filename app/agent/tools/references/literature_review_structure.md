# Literature Review Structure

Extracted from academic-research-skills (ARS) by Imbad0202.

## Standard Literature Review Architecture

### 1. Abstract
- Background and rationale: 1-2 sentences
- Purpose and scope: 1 sentence
- Method (search strategy): 1 sentence
- Key findings from synthesis: 2-3 sentences
- Implications and identified gaps: 1-2 sentences

### 2. Introduction
- Topic and rationale: why is this review needed?
- Scope and boundaries: time period, disciplines, geographies
- Review methodology: search strategy, databases, inclusion/exclusion criteria, number of sources
- Organization of the review: preview thematic structure

### 3. Thematic Body (3-5 themes)
Each theme:
- 2-3 sub-themes with citations
- Summary of what the literature collectively tells us about this theme
- Synthesis NOT serial summaries

### 4. Cross-Cutting Synthesis
- Convergent findings: what themes agree on, patterns across themes
- Divergent findings and debates: unresolved disagreements
- Methodological observations: dominant/underused methods

### 5. Research Gaps and Future Directions
- 3-5 specific gaps
- Proposed research agenda: methods, datasets, directions

### 6. Conclusion
- 3-5 key takeaways
- Implications for practice/policy

## Writing Quality Checklist

From ARS writing quality standards:
- Avoid overused AI-typical terms (delve, tapestry, landscape, etc.)
- Avoid em dash overuse — prefer commas, parentheses, or shorter sentences
- Avoid throat-clearing openers (It is worth noting that, Interestingly, etc.)
- Vary paragraph lengths (not all 3-5 sentences)
- Vary sentence rhythm (not all compound-complex)
- Each paragraph: one controlling idea + support
- Citations must be real and verified — never invent references
- Every major claim needs evidence from the knowledge base

## Literature Strategist Principles

- Design systematic search strategies before writing
- Screen sources with inclusion/exclusion criteria
- Create annotated bibliographies grouped by theme
- Build literature matrices for cross-comparison
- Never write paper-by-paper summaries — always synthesize thematically
